\begin{answer}
Since $p(y;\eta)$ is a valid distribution, $\int p(y;\eta)\,dy = 1$. Differentiating both sides w.r.t.\ $\eta$ and exchanging the derivative and integral (as given by the hint):
\[
0 = \int \frac{\partial}{\partial\eta} p(y;\eta)\,dy
 = \int \frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\bigl[b(y)\exp(\eta y - a(\eta))\bigr]\,dy
 = \int p(y;\eta)\bigl(y - a'(\eta)\bigr)\,dy.
\]
Splitting the integral:
\[
\int p(y;\eta)\,y\,dy = a'(\eta)\int p(y;\eta)\,dy = a'(\eta).
\]
Hence $\mathbb{E}[Y;\eta] = \dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta}a(\eta)$. $\blacksquare$
\end{answer}

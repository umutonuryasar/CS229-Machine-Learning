\begin{answer}
By Bayes' rule:
$$p(\theta \mid x, y) = \frac{p(y \mid x, \theta)\, p(\theta \mid x)}{p(y \mid x)}.$$
Since $p(y \mid x)$ does not depend on $\theta$, maximizing over $\theta$ gives:
$$\theta_{\mathrm{MAP}} = \operatorname*{argmax}_\theta\; p(y \mid x, \theta)\, p(\theta \mid x).$$
Under the assumption $p(\theta) = p(\theta \mid x)$ (i.e., the prior on $\theta$ does not change when we condition on the inputs $x$):
$$\theta_{\mathrm{MAP}} = \operatorname*{argmax}_\theta\; p(y \mid x, \theta)\, p(\theta). \qquad \square$$
\end{answer}

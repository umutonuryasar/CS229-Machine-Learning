\begin{answer}
From part (a), $\theta_{\mathrm{MAP}} = \operatorname*{argmax}_\theta\; p(y|x,\theta)\,p(\theta)$. Taking the log (a monotone transform):
$$\theta_{\mathrm{MAP}} = \operatorname*{argmax}_\theta\; \bigl[\log p(y|x,\theta) + \log p(\theta)\bigr].$$

With the Gaussian prior $\theta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\eta^2 I)$:
$$\log p(\theta) = -\frac{d}{2}\log(2\pi\eta^2) - \frac{\|\theta\|_2^2}{2\eta^2}.$$

Dropping constants (which do not affect the argmax):
$$\theta_{\mathrm{MAP}} = \operatorname*{argmax}_\theta\; \left[\log p(y|x,\theta) - \frac{\|\theta\|_2^2}{2\eta^2}\right] = \operatorname*{argmin}_\theta\; \left[-\log p(y|x,\theta) + \frac{1}{2\eta^2}\|\theta\|_2^2\right].$$

This matches the form of Equation~\eqref{eqn:1} with
$$\boxed{\lambda = \frac{1}{2\eta^2}.}$$
Thus MAP estimation with a zero-mean Gaussian prior is equivalent to MLE with $L_2$ regularization. $\square$
\end{answer}

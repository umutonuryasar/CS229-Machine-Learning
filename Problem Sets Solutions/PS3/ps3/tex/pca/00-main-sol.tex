\begin{answer}
The projection of $x^{(i)}$ onto the direction $u$ is
$$f_u(x^{(i)}) = (u^T x^{(i)})\,u,$$
since $u$ is a unit vector. The squared reconstruction error for one point is
\begin{align*}
\|x^{(i)} - f_u(x^{(i)})\|_2^2
&= \|x^{(i)} - (u^T x^{(i)})u\|_2^2 \\
&= \|x^{(i)}\|_2^2 - 2(u^T x^{(i)})^2 + (u^T x^{(i)})^2 \|u\|^2 \\
&= \|x^{(i)}\|_2^2 - (u^T x^{(i)})^2,
\end{align*}
using $\|u\|^2 = u^Tu = 1$.

Summing over all examples:
$$\sum_{i=1}^\nexp \|x^{(i)} - f_u(x^{(i)})\|_2^2 = \sum_{i=1}^\nexp \|x^{(i)}\|_2^2 - \sum_{i=1}^\nexp (u^T x^{(i)})^2.$$

The first term is constant w.r.t.\ $u$, so minimizing the total squared error is equivalent to maximizing:
$$\sum_{i=1}^\nexp (u^T x^{(i)})^2 = \sum_{i=1}^\nexp u^T x^{(i)}(x^{(i)})^T u = u^T \!\left(\sum_{i=1}^\nexp x^{(i)}(x^{(i)})^T\right)\! u = u^T \Sigma u,$$
where $\Sigma = \sum_{i=1}^\nexp x^{(i)}(x^{(i)})^T$ is proportional to the sample covariance matrix (data are zero-mean by assumption).

Therefore:
$$\arg\min_{u:\,u^Tu=1}\sum_{i=1}^\nexp \|x^{(i)}-f_u(x^{(i)})\|_2^2 = \arg\max_{u:\,u^Tu=1}\, u^T\Sigma u.$$

By the Rayleigh quotient theorem, the maximizer of $u^T\Sigma u$ subject to $u^Tu=1$ is the eigenvector of $\Sigma$ corresponding to its largest eigenvalue, which is exactly the \textbf{first principal component}. $\square$
\end{answer}
